Japanese-Donated Park to Become Public Fitness Hub

A three-hectare park in Phnom Penh’s Sangkat Choam Chaov II, donated by a Japanese couple in 2022, will be renovated into a public green space designed to support exercise and outdoor activities. Photo: Phnom Penh City Hall

PHNOM PENH – A three-hectare park in Phnom Penh’s Sangkat Choam Chaov II, donated by a Japanese couple in 2022, will be renovated into a public green space designed to support exercise and outdoor activities.

An urban planning expert says the project will benefit from incorporating local residents’ ideas into its design and management.

During a visit to the site on July 13, Phnom Penh Governor Khuong Sreng said the park would be upgraded with additional trees, seating, toilets and trash bins to make it more comfortable for visitors.

“The routes surrounded by trees must be concreted to a width of eight meters,” he said. “The renovation will be completed by late 2026 or early 2027.”

Phnom Penh City Hall says the park will be open to the public and is intended to promote physical activity and community wellbeing.

Ses Aronsakda, a research fellow in urban planning at Future Forum, welcomed the initiative and said the park could become a model for community-centered public space if authorities involve residents in the planning.

“The park could be the first to be designed, planned and operated with input from the locals themselves, who will be its future users,” he said.

Aronsakda noted that even relatively small green spaces can provide significant environmental and health benefits, especially in rapidly growing urban areas.

“Every square meter of green space brings health and environmental benefits. This is why smaller pocket parks are often the preferred way to add green spaces into a crowded city,” he said.

He added that a three-hectare park would provide ample space for exercise and recreation while accommodating many visitors.

Involving residents at an early stage could help ensure that the park reflects the area's character, addresses community concerns and meets users’ needs.

He spoke of the importance of thoughtfully managing street vendors, arguing that they are an integral part of public spaces.

“Vendors are the lifeblood of public spaces. Without them, a city's public realm will be far too sterile, empty and unsafe,” he said.

The land was donated in 2022 by Michiko Kansaki, wife of Koji Kanzaki. The couple was said to have a deep affection for Cambodia and its natural environment, and offered the park as a gift to the capital for public use. Photo: Phnom Penh City Hall

Rather than excluding vendors, Aronsakda suggested designating specific areas for them and providing facilities such as seating, waste collection points and cleaning services.

“With this system in place, the park can maintain its function and hygiene while also accommodating vendors,” he said.

The land was donated in 2022 by Michiko Kansaki, wife of Koji Kanzaki. The couple was said to have a deep affection for Cambodia and its natural environment, and offered the park as a gift to the capital for public use.
